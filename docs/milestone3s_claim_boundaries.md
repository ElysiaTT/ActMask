# Milestone 3S claim boundaries

## Supported claims

- Ordinary dynamic action-verification setups can admit static or order-invariant shortcuts.
- Matched counterfactual construction removes the tested static shortcut; signed-order pairs force temporal direction in the frozen 3Q tasks.
- In the frozen state-only nonlinear v2 protocol, earlier history beyond the final two frames is necessary for the reported verifier performance.
- The lightweight GRU exceeds the strongest selected fair analytic baseline by +0.400 ID pair-order accuracy with grouped CI [0.3951, 0.4052], and has positive advantage on all three frozen OOD axes.
- The v2 candidate protocol is action-diverse in the audited full data, and C20 inference is comfortably above 5 Hz (p95 0.816 ms).

## Weak or limited claims

- All evidence uses state observations and procedural GPU-PhysX executions.
- The learned verifier is lightweight; it is neither a VLA nor an end-to-end robot policy.
- The benchmark tests a specified shortcut taxonomy, not every possible shortcut.
- The results do not evaluate visual perception, tracking, external assets, or pretrained models.

## Prohibited claims

Do not claim real-robot success, RGB-D success, visual tracking success, universal dynamics understanding, superiority over all robot policies, a new large-scale VLA model, or end-to-end manipulation-policy improvement.

## Future-work claims

Future work may evaluate RGB-D state estimation, tracking/correspondence ambiguity, recorded or real-robot trajectories, richer object interactions, and policy integration. Each needs a separately preregistered protocol and authorization.
