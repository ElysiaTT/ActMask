\begin{abstract}
High performance on a dynamic robot-action verification task does not establish that a verifier uses dynamics: labels can correlate with current geometry, candidate templates, unordered motion summaries, or a short suffix of the history. We present \bench, an execution-grounded counterfactual evaluation ladder for diagnosing these shortcuts. The ladder first matches static information, then matches unordered history statistics while changing temporal direction, and finally constructs nonlinear counterfactual pairs with matched final two frames, a valid observable pair-swap, action-diverse candidates, and independent out-of-distribution (OOD) axes. Labels are obtained from GPU-PhysX executions rather than from the formulas used by fair analytic estimators. In the frozen final state-only protocol, a lightweight GRU exceeds the validation-selected fair analytic baseline by 0.400 pair-order accuracy (grouped 95\% interval [0.3951, 0.4052]) and has positive advantage on physical-parameter, temporal-delay, and held-mechanism OOD tests. Audits report exact pair swaps, balanced candidate templates, grouped splits, and no blocking leakage finding. These results establish a controlled state-observation benchmark result, not RGB-D perception, real-robot transfer, or end-to-end policy improvement.
\end{abstract}
